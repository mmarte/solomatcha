A matcha tin is small, and most of what is printed on it is marketing. Five lines carry real information. Learn those and you can judge a tin in under a minute.

[![An example matcha tin label with five numbered callouts: one ingredient, a named origin, a harvest or best-by date, the grade word, and bright green color](/assets/img/label-anatomy.svg)](/assets/img/label-anatomy.svg)

*The five lines to read, numbered in the order of the sections below. Tap the picture to enlarge it.*

## 1. The ingredient list

Pure matcha has one ingredient: matcha, which is powdered tencha leaf. If you see sugar, milk powder, creamer, flavoring or "natural flavors", you are looking at a drink mix. Mixes can be fine for a sweet latte, but they are priced like matcha and should not be.

## 2. The origin

A good label names a region or prefecture, such as Uji (Kyoto), Nishio (Aichi), Yame (Fukuoka) or Kagoshima. "Imported from Japan" is a start, but it is not an origin. "Product of Asia" tells you nothing.

## 3. The harvest or best-by date

Matcha changes as it sits. A seller that prints a harvest year or a best-by date is tracking its stock. A tin with no date at all gives you no way to judge how long it has been on the shelf.

## 4. The grade word

"Ceremonial", "premium", "daily" and "culinary" are seller terms. There is no single legal grading scale behind them, so the same word can mean different things at different shops. Treat the word as a hint about intended use, then check the other four lines. Our [grades guide](/guides/matcha-grades-explained/) goes through this in more detail.

## 5. The color and texture you can see

If the shop shows the powder or a photo of a prepared cup, look for a bright green and a fine, even texture. Dull yellow or brown powder suggests old leaf or a poor blend.

## A quick checklist

| Line | Looks good | Walk away if |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredients | Matcha only | Sugar, milk, flavor |
| Origin | A named region | "Imported" only |
| Date | Harvest or best-by stated | No date anywhere |
| Grade word | Matches the use you want | Used as the only reason to pay more |
| Color | Bright, even green | Dull, yellow or brown |

## A real example: what one premium seller prints

Ippodo's US store lists its Ummon matcha at $81 for a 40 g bag (checked November 15, 2026). The product page says the tea is "freshly packaged in Japan" and gives a best-before date of 180 days from packaging, with advice to finish it within about a month of opening. It does not name a growing region or a harvest year on that page.

That is a useful reminder that even a respected seller may print only some of the five lines on a product page. When a line is missing, check the tin itself or ask the seller. Do not assume.
